# Wildforge — Living Game Design

## Vision

Wildforge is an original, low-poly voxel survival sandbox for the browser. Its opening baseline should feel immediately legible to players of block-building games: generate a world, explore, gather, craft, survive, and reshape every part of the landscape. Once that foundation is dependable, the game will develop its own creatures, resources, forging systems, visual language, and progression.

Wildforge will not copy proprietary source code, textures, sounds, names, characters, or branding. All shipped assets and implementations will be original.

## Design pillars

1. **Everything is shapeable.** Terrain and player structures use the same persistent block system.
2. **Every expedition has a purpose.** Biomes, depths, weather, and creatures supply distinct resources.
3. **Building is progression.** A home evolves into a workshop, settlement, and eventually a magical-industrial forge.
4. **Readable, tactile graphics.** Chunky silhouettes, crisp pixel-inspired surfaces, strong daylight, and satisfying particles keep the world clear.
5. **A world worth keeping.** Seeds are reproducible and player edits, inventories, structures, and entities persist.

## Product scope

### World

- Seeded procedural terrain, effectively unbounded on the horizontal plane
- Streaming chunks, caves, ores, aquifers, biome blending, structures, and dimensions/realms
- Day/night cycle, weather, ambient effects, fluid simulation, fire, and vegetation growth
- Persistent edits and deterministic generation independent of traversal order

### Player

- First-person walking, sprinting, crouching, jumping, swimming, climbing, and fall damage
- Health, hunger, armor, status effects, respawning, and configurable controls
- Mining reach, block placement rules, tool effectiveness, and tactile feedback

### Items and progression

- Data-driven block and item registry
- Stackable inventory, hotbar, containers, equipment, durability, and item drops
- Shaped/shapeless crafting, furnaces, fuel, cooking, repair, enchanting, and Wildforge-specific forging
- Progression from gathered wood and stone through metals, rare materials, automation, and realm resources

### Living world

- Passive wildlife, neutral creatures, hostile enemies, bosses, spawning rules, navigation, combat, and loot
- Trees, crops, fungi, flowers, rocks, mineral deposits, ruins, villages, and dungeons
- Farming, breeding, fishing, trade, and settlement systems

### Building

- Placeable structural and decorative blocks
- Doors, stairs, slabs, fences, lights, signs, beds, storage, workstations, and interactive mechanisms
- Stable schema-v3 save envelopes with automatic migrations, player/edit recovery copies, storage-failure isolation, and transactional complete-world export/import

## Delivery roadmap

### Milestone 1 — Playable voxel foundation

Seeded overworld, chunk streaming, original procedural block colors, first-person controller, collision, mining, placement, hotbar, and local persistence.

### Milestone 2 — Survival loop

Item drops, inventory UI, recipes, crafting table, tools, durability, health, hunger, daylight, basic hostile creature, death, and respawn.

### Milestone 3 — World depth

Multiple biomes, improved noise and decoration, caves, ore veins, fluids, weather, structures, ambient sound, and world creation UI.

### Milestone 4 — Living ecosystem

Entity framework, passive and hostile families, navigation, combat variety, farming, breeding, fishing, and loot tables.

### Milestone 5 — Builder's world

Expanded block catalog, orientation and block states, stairs/slabs/fences, doors, storage, lighting propagation, redstone-inspired mechanisms, and blueprints.

### Milestone 6 — The Wildforge identity

Distinct art direction, magical metallurgy, specialized biomes and realms, signature enemies, lore structures, advanced forging, and settlement progression.

### Milestone 7 — Scale and polish

Greedy meshing, worker-thread generation, robust save migrations, soundscape, accessibility, settings, performance tiers, automated testing, and optional multiplayer research.

## Milestone 1 acceptance criteria

- A new seed produces a deterministic landscape.
- The player can walk, sprint, jump, collide with terrain, and use pointer-lock mouse look.
- Nearby chunks appear as the player travels and distant chunks are removed.
- The player can target, break, collect conceptually, select, and place common blocks.
- Reloading the same seed preserves edited blocks in local browser storage.
- `npm run build` completes without errors.

## Engineering direction

- Three.js renderer with custom chunk geometry rather than one mesh per block
- ES modules and Vite for a small, transparent browser toolchain
- Data-oriented block registry and deterministic world-generation functions
- Separate simulation, rendering, input, UI, and persistence boundaries as systems grow
- Original generated textures/materials; no third-party game assets required for the baseline
